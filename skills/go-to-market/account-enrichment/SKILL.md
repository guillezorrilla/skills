# Account enrichment

## Overview

Enrichment fails in two directions. It produces a page of company trivia nobody reads, or it produces one confident sentence that is false, "congratulations on the funding round" when there was no round, which ends the relationship before it starts.

Both come from the same gap. A dossier is not a summary of a company. It is the evidence behind one decision: is there a reason to contact this account now, and does this person own the problem? Every line either changes that decision or does not belong, and every line carries where it came from and when.

The other habit worth building: look inside before looking outside. The CRM, the last email thread and the notes from the last call usually hold more than the open web does, and they are the facts a prospect or customer actually remembers.

## When to Use

- Before a first touch, so the opening line can be built on something real.
- Before a renewal, an expansion conversation, or a QBR.
- Before a call, when the other side will expect you to have looked.
- An account went quiet and someone needs to know what changed.
- Asked what is going on at a company, or who to talk to there.

**Not for:** building the target list in the first place; general company reading with no decision attached; or profiling a private individual. This covers companies, and people in their professional capacity.

## Work inside the team's setup

Start from what the team already has. If they keep a process doc, a brief or an account-planning template in whatever they use for documents, read it. If their CRM, inbox or notes tool is connected, open those before the web, because they hold the things the customer actually remembers.

Two things change the output and are worth establishing either way: how account ownership gets checked, and what their support desk counts as an escalation. Where you cannot establish one, name the gap in the dossier rather than working around it silently.

## Look inside first

Whatever tools are connected, check them before searching the web. In practice that means:

- The CRM, HubSpot, Salesforce or whatever the team runs: owner, stage, every previous touch, closed-lost reasons, and whether someone else is already working this account.
- Email history: who spoke to them, when, what was asked, what was promised, and whether they ever replied.
- Meeting notes and call recordings from tools such as Granola, Fireflies, Gong or Notion: what they said in their own words, which is worth more than anything you can infer from outside.
- Support tickets, product usage and billing, where the account is already a customer.
- Shared drives and wikis, for the proposal or pricing that already went out.

Two rules that come with internal data. First, it is evidence and not gospel, so date it and attribute it the same as anything else: "call note, 2026-05-12, from Ana" tells the reader how much to trust it. Second, notes written in confidence stay internal. What a contact said privately about their vendor, their budget or their boss is context for your decision, never a line you quote back to them.

Check ownership early. Discovering that a colleague has an open thread, or that the account is in someone else's sequence, changes the whole task and takes one lookup.

When a tool is not connected, say so as a gap. "No CRM access, so previous contact history is unknown" is useful. A plausible guess in its place is not.

## The Process

### 1. Name the decision, then gather only what serves it

A first-touch dossier, a renewal dossier and a competitive-displacement dossier hold different facts, and a page trying to serve all three serves none. For every line ask whether the opposite fact would change what you do. If not, cut it.

### 2. Facts from primary sources, dated

Prefer what the company itself publishes: its site, product and pricing pages, app-store listings, careers page, filings, release notes. Second-hand coverage points you at the primary source rather than replacing it. A press release is the company's own claim, which is fine as long as it is labelled that way.

Do not answer from memory. A model's recollection of a company is stale by construction and confidently wrong about the details that make a message land: headcount, funding, leadership, which product they actually sell. Look it up and record what you looked at.

```
Verified    · 34 locations · their store locator, checked 2026-08-06
Verified    · iOS app last updated 2026-07-29 · App Store listing
Their claim · "fastest growing in the region" · press release, 2026-06-02
Internal    · asked about multi-location reporting · call note, 2026-05-12, Ana
Inferred    · runs kitchen ops in house · from 3 ops job posts, Jul 2026
Unknown     · current POS vendor · not stated in any source available
```

### 3. Hunt triggers, stalled attempts, and absences

A trigger is an observable change that makes now different from last quarter: funding or an acquisition, a leadership hire or departure in the relevant function, a new location or market or product, a hiring pattern that implies a project, a pricing change, a visible technology change, a regulatory deadline that lands on them, something a named executive said publicly, an expiring contract or a public RFP. Internally, a promise made and now due is also a trigger, and a better one than most.

Age matters. Inside about 90 days it is a trigger. Older than that it is context, and congratulating someone on an eighteen-month-old announcement tells them exactly how the message was generated.

Finding no trigger is itself a finding. Write it down rather than promoting a weak one: "no dated trigger found, the reason to contact them is segment fit, which is thinner" is an honest and useful sentence.

**A stalled attempt beats a trigger.** Look for the thing they started and did not finish: a feature live at four locations out of twenty-nine, an app listing their own site links to nowhere, a page that still works but is reachable only by direct URL, a listing marked inactive. Those are worth more than any announcement, because they prove both that someone wanted this and that the attempt ran out of road. Record where it is live, where it is not, and the date you checked.

**Absence is evidence.** What a company is not doing is checkable and often decisive. Zero relevant roles open anywhere in their hiring system says something about priority. A capability their competitors all have and they do not is a gap you can point at. Two years of announcements with nothing in the category you sell is a pattern. Say what you looked for and did not find, not just what you found.

### 4. People, in professional capacity only

Role, scope, tenure, what they have said publicly about their priorities, and what they are hiring for. Their own words outrank anything inferred, so look for the talk, the post, the interview, the job description they wrote, the thing they said on your own last call.

Stay inside the line: no inferred personality, nothing about their politics, health, family, religion or any other protected or private matter, and nothing from their personal life. Aside from being wrong, it turns a business message into an intrusion.

When seniority or ownership is unclear, record it as unknown rather than rounding up so the page looks better.

**Check names against a primary source, not an aggregator.** Directories, data vendors and old articles keep stale titles alive for years, and addressing someone by a job they left in 2021 is the most expensive small error in this whole process. Confirm against the company's own site, a filing, or something they published themselves. Where no current holder of a role is publicly identifiable, that is the answer, and it is more useful than a confident wrong name.

### 5. Label every line verified, internal, inferred or unknown

The split is the whole value. Verified carries a source and a date. Internal carries the tool, the date and the person. Inferred names what it was inferred from, so the next reader can disagree with the reasoning instead of inheriting the conclusion. Unknown stays unknown, because a dossier with honest gaps is usable and one that fills gaps silently is a trap.

Nothing labelled inferred may be said to the customer as fact. That single rule is what stops enrichment turning into fabrication one step downstream.

### 6. Treat a contradiction as a finding

Two sources disagreeing about headcount, funding or who runs a function is not a coin flip. Prefer the primary and more recent one, and say the other exists. The disagreement is often the most interesting thing on the page, because it usually means something changed.

### 7. End with what the next person actually needs

Four short things, and nothing else:

- **Why now**, in one sentence, naming the dated trigger or admitting there is not one.
- **What would disqualify them**, the observable that means do not bother. It stops a weak account being carried forward on optimism.
- **The objection you will hit**, named in their words. Almost every account already solves this somehow, even if the answer is a spreadsheet, and "we already have that" is the reply you should be ready for rather than surprised by. Say what the status quo is and what it costs them.
- **What not to say.** Live redundancies, a labour dispute, litigation, a public failure, a competitor they lost to. A rep who walks into one of those loses the account in a sentence, and it takes ten minutes to find them.

Then a short list of what to re-check immediately before the call, because a dossier ages the moment it is written and the leadership line is the first thing to move.

If the dossier runs longer than the message it exists to inform, it has failed at its job.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I know this company." | You know a stale snapshot, wrong on exactly the details that make a message land. Look it up today. |
| "I'll start with a web search." | Start with the CRM, the last thread and the last call note. The web does not know what you already promised them. |
| "It was true last time I checked." | Then it needs a fresh date or it is not verified. Leadership and headcount are the fields that move. |
| "Close enough to a trigger, they're clearly growing." | Not dated, not observable. Name the event and its date or say there is no trigger. |
| "More context is better." | A page nobody reads costs the same as one nobody wrote. Every line must be able to change the decision. |
| "The article says they raised." | The article is a lead. Find the filing or their own announcement, or label it as reported. |
| "I'll mark it verified and check later." | Verified is a claim someone repeats to a customer. Later does not arrive. |
| "Their title says VP, so they own it." | A title is not scope. Unclear ownership is an unknown. |
| "The call note says they hate their vendor, that's a great opener." | It was said in confidence. Internal notes inform your decision, they do not get quoted back. |
| "A personal detail warms it up." | It reads as surveillance, and some of it is unlawful to use. Professional capacity only. |
| "Both sources are plausible, I'll pick one." | Pick the primary and say the other exists. The disagreement is usually the story. |
| "No CRM access, I'll assume this is a fresh account." | Assume that and you email into a colleague's live deal. Record the gap instead. |
| "There's no trigger, so there's nothing here." | Look for the stalled attempt instead. A half-finished rollout is stronger than an announcement, because someone already wanted it and it stopped. |
| "I only report what I found." | What you looked for and did not find is a finding. No relevant roles open anywhere is a fact about priority. |
| "Their profile says they're the CEO." | Aggregators keep stale titles alive for years. Confirm against the company's own source, or record the role as unknown. |
| "The objection is obvious, I'll leave it out." | Then the rep meets it live and improvises. Name the status quo and what it costs them. |
| "Landmines aren't my job." | Ten minutes of looking prevents the one sentence that ends the account. |

## Red Flags

- Any factual claim with no date.
- The word "recently" doing the work a date should do.
- Facts that exist only in your summary and in no source you can point at.
- Inferences written in the same voice as verified facts.
- A trigger older than a quarter presented as news.
- Web research done while a connected CRM or inbox went unchecked.
- No check on whether a colleague already owns the account.
- A private call note quoted as if it were public.
- Anything about a person's character or private life.
- Answered from memory with no lookup.
- No "why now" line, or one with nothing observable in it.
- No disqualifier, or a dossier longer than the email it feeds.
- A named executive taken from an aggregator with no primary-source check.
- Only positives recorded, with no note of what was looked for and not found.
- No objection anticipated, so the rep meets "we already do this" cold.
- No landmines checked, in an account with any public trouble.
- Nothing to re-check before the call, in a dossier that will be read days later.

- [ ] Account ownership and any live thread or sequence were checked
- [ ] Every claim is labelled verified, internal, inferred or unknown
- [ ] Every verified claim carries a source and the date it was checked
- [ ] Internal claims carry the tool, the date and who said it
- [ ] Every inference names what it was inferred from
- [ ] Triggers carry dates, and stale ones are labelled context
- [ ] "No trigger found" was stated where that was the truth
- [ ] Nothing said in confidence is set up to be quoted back to them
- [ ] People are described in professional capacity only
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are surfaced, not silently resolved
- [ ] Any stalled or partial rollout was looked for, with where it is and is not live
- [ ] What was looked for and not found is recorded, not just what was found
- [ ] Names and titles were confirmed against a primary source, or recorded as unknown
- [ ] The page ends with why now, the disqualifier, the objection, and what not to say
- [ ] A short re-check list is attached for immediately before the call
- [ ] Gaps caused by missing tools or access are named as gaps
